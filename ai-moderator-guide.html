A2 Unit Away Day · Moderator Guide

Working with AI,
hands on.

Moderators: Fatime & Valentina Audience: A2 unit colleagues Setting: Away day · playful, hands-on
The aim of the session

To grow the A2 unit's knowledge and confident use of AI — during the away day, in a playful way. Less lecture, more experience: the intention is that colleagues leave more curious, and with some hands-on materials they can start implementing the following day.

00
Opening

Opening the room

~10 min
A few minutes to open the room — curiosity over fear.
Purpose
  • Open the room and set a warm, human tone.
  • Turn any fear or scepticism into curiosity.
  • Frame the day: AI expands us, it doesn't replace us.
What it is

A short, human opening — creating curiosity and highlighting how tools don't shrink us, they expand us. It sets the tone for everything after: curiosity over fear, judgement over hype.

Who does what

★
Warm-up · on our feet

Where do we stand?

~12 min
Where do we stand? Stand where you feel.
Purpose
  • Energise the group and break the ice.
  • Surface the room's diverse perspectives on AI.
  • Create a shared starting point for the day.
Method — spectrum positioning

People place themselves along an imaginary line (or in a corner) to show where they stand. It gets bodies moving and makes the room's range visible.

Round 1 · how much do you use it?
"I've already used AI for something — at work or at home."
All the timeNever touched it
Round 2 · four corners

The same question, one layer deeper. Two axes: how much you use AI (a lot / a little) × how well you know its risks (yes / not really). Go to the corner that fits.

Uses a lot · doesn't know the risks
Uses a little · knows the risks

Round 3 · do you trust it?
"AI is a brilliant assistant… or a confident liar that makes things up."
Brilliant assistantConfident fibber
Round 4 · where is this going?
"In two years, AI will feel like a trusted colleague here at HaDEA."
AbsolutelyStill a side experiment
Who does what

Fatime introduces each question and leads the rounds.

For the four corners, Fatime and Valentina take opposite corners of the room to anchor what each corner means.

Once people have placed themselves, Valentina asks a few of them why they positioned themselves there (about a minute each).

Materials: four flipcharts — one to label each corner.

01
Together · quiz

How wrong are we about AI?

~10 min
Phones out — how wrong are we about AI?
Purpose
  • Meet our own assumptions about AI — and watch a few of them fall.
  • Set up the day's real lesson: we can be fluent, confident, and still wrong.
How it runs
  1. People join the quiz on their phones (Slido).
  2. One question at a time: everyone answers, then we reveal.
  3. After each reveal, a quick word on why the common answer is wrong — light, curious, never a lecture.
Who does what

Fatime prepares the questions on Slido, introduces the set-up, and reads the questions and answers.

Valentina runs the mini-reflection series — a short feedback moment or mini-interview after each question, or after every two.

02
Together · build it live

Build a great prompt, step by step

~15 min
Same task — add a layer, watch it get better.
Purpose

Show how much the answer improves when the prompt is built with care — and send everyone away with a recipe they can reuse.

What we cover

Two ladders, both from our own work. Each starts with a lazy prompt and climbs to a usable one, re-running at every step so the room sees the jump. The last rung is always the same: a good prompt fixes structure and voice, but only checking fixes facts.

03
Hands-on · teams

Prompt battle

~15 min
Same recipe, new subject — the funniest prompt wins.
Purpose

Put the prompt structure to work — playfully, in teams.

The exercise — explain HaDEA three ways

Each team gets one audience and writes one prompt explaining what HaDEA does to that audience. Same subject for everyone; only the audience changes.

  1. Teams open the team sheet and pick their audience (5 min to write).
  2. Each team reads out their prompt, then the answer it produced.
  3. The room votes. The richest prompt almost always wins.
  • A five-year-old — who has never heard the word "agency".
  • A sceptical minister — twenty seconds, in a lift.
  • A human in 2040 — explained by an alien who has been watching us all along.
Handout · teams work from this Prompt Battle — Team Sheet

The prompt structure with the given parts filled in and the spaces where each team's own ideas go. Everyone opens it at the start of the session.

Who does what

Fatime introduces the challenge.

Valentina forms the groups (or pre-forms them before the event), moderates, invites volunteers to present, and runs the vote.

A recurring task, solved

The task you do again and again — solved.
Purpose

To respond to the needs colleagues expressed in the survey.

What we'll do

We work through the areas that came back most often — using real tasks, not hypothetical ones:

Split still to confirm.

Framing · inspiration

Use cases — from outside work

~20 min
The breadth — health, apps, knowledge, keynotes, video.
Purpose

See the breadth — then find what fits your week. Use cases from outside work, to open people's imagination to what's possible.

  • Support on health and nutrition
  • Connected apps

  • Keynote writing

Who does what
