Your GPT@EC+ starter kit

GPT@EC+ can draft your emails — but it works far better once you tell it how you write. This takes about five minutes: first you teach it your style, then you save a set of ready-made prompts for everyday emails. Everything highlighted in yellow is yours to change.

1

Teach it your style

In the plug-in, open GPT@EC+ control → Settings, switch on “Use custom instructions”, and paste the text below into the box.

Use custom instructions← turn this on
Write polite, kind, and warm emails that are short and to the point. Keep the tone human and friendly, never stiff or corporate — avoid jargon and filler. Use clear, simple language and short sentences. Start every email with a greeting in the form: "Dear [name],". When the email has several points, present them as a list using square bullet points (▪). Put key information in bold so it stands out — in particular dates, deadlines, deliverables, and status words such as "approved" or "suspended". End every email with: Have a lovely day, [Your name] For formal or sensitive emails, use this sign-off instead: Kind regards, [Your name]
What to change:
  • Everything in yellow is a style choice — make it your own: how short, the greeting, the bullet style, what to put in bold.
  • Replace [Your name] with your name — in both places.
  • Keep the “Kind regards” fallback — it saves you from a cheerful sign-off on serious emails.
2

Save your ready-made prompts

Open Personal Prompts → “Save this personal prompt”, type the Title, paste the Prompt, select “Reply”, and click Save — then repeat for each one below.

Draft Reply← select Reply

Click a title to open the prompt, then copy it.

Everyday emails
Reply to a meeting request›
Draft a short, warm reply to a meeting invitation. Confirm availability, or propose two or three alternative slots if the time doesn't work. Ask for an agenda or the purpose if none is given. Keep it brief.
Gentle deadline reminder›
Draft a short, friendly reminder about an upcoming deadline. State clearly what is due and by when, offer help if useful, and keep the tone kind and non-pushy. Leave a placeholder for [deliverable] and [deadline].
Thanks / noted›
Draft a very short, warm reply acknowledging the message and confirming it is noted. Thank the sender, and mention any next step only if it's relevant. Keep it to a few lines.
Request information or document›
Draft a short, polite request asking the recipient to provide a specific [document / information]. Say clearly what is needed and, if there is a deadline, by when. Keep it warm and easy to say yes to.
Confirm next steps (with bullets)›
Draft a short, warm email confirming the next steps after [meeting / decision]. List the actions as square bullets, noting who does what and by when. Bold the dates and deadlines. Keep it clear and brief.
Follow up on no reply›
Draft a short, friendly follow-up on a previous email that hasn't had a reply. Reference the earlier message, gently restate what's needed and any deadline, and assume good faith — no reproach. Keep it light and brief.
Polite decline›
Draft a short, kind reply that declines a request or invitation. Be warm and appreciative, give a brief reason if appropriate, and offer an alternative or a pointer to someone else if possible. Keep it gracious and brief.
Introduction email›
Draft a short, warm introduction connecting two people. Say who each person is in one line, why they should connect, and hand over so they can take it from there. Keep it brief.
Thank you after a meeting›
Draft a short, warm thank-you after a meeting or call. Thank them for their time, note one or two key points or agreed next steps as square bullets, and confirm who does what by when. Keep it brief.
Share a document for review›
Draft a short, warm email sharing a document for review. Say what it is, what feedback you're looking for, and by when. Keep it clear and brief, and leave a placeholder for [document] and [deadline].
Limited availability notice›
Draft a short, warm note letting the recipient know you'll have limited availability for a period. Give the dates, say who to contact in the meantime or when you'll respond, and keep it reassuring and brief. Leave placeholders for [dates] and [backup contact].
Project & payment workflow — example set, adapt to your team
Acknowledge report received›
Draft a short, warm reply confirming we have received the report. Thank the sender, confirm the report title and the date received, and say we will review it and come back if anything is needed. Keep it brief.
Acknowledge invoice received›
Draft a short, warm reply confirming we have received the invoice. Thank the sender, confirm the invoice reference and date received, and note that it will be processed according to the usual procedure. Keep it brief, and avoid committing to a specific payment date unless one is given.
Interim report accepted → next steps›
Draft a warm, professional email informing the recipient that we have no further comments on the [first / second] interim report and that it is accepted. Then set out the next steps as square bullets: ▪ mark the cover page and header/footer with the status "Final version" and today's date; ▪ upload the Word and PDF versions to CIRCABC; ▪ send the request for the [first] interim payment, with all supporting documents, to HaDEA-BTSF-PROJECTS@ec.europa.eu as soon as possible. Thank them for their cooperation and invite them to contact us for any further information. Bold "accepted", "Final version", and any dates.
Missing invoice — urgent chase›
Draft a polite but clearly urgent email noting that we have not yet received the invoice for the [TIP] for [project reference]. Explain that the report was accepted in [month/year] and that HaDEA plans to execute the payment in [year], so the invoice and supporting documents are needed as soon as possible. Ask the recipient to acknowledge receipt and submit the invoice to HaDEA-BTSF-PROJECTS@ec.europa.eu. Bold the deadline pressure and the acceptance date.
Prepare payment workflow (Ares)›
Draft a warm, collegial internal email to colleagues sharing an invoice and supporting documents for the [third] interim payment of [project reference]. Present the key references as square bullets: ▪ the payment request registered in Ares under [Ares reference], with a CF assignment provided to [ve_hadea.operational invoice]; ▪ the acceptance of the report registered in Ares under [Ares reference], with both files linked. Ask them to prepare the payment workflow in Ares at their earliest convenience, and to put my name as project adviser. Bold the Ares references.
Payment suspension notice›
Draft a clear, professional, and courteous email informing the recipient that the payment for [project / contract reference] is suspended. State plainly that it is suspended and explain the reason briefly and factually: [reason]. Set out, as square bullets, what is needed to lift the suspension: ▪ [action or document]; ▪ [action or document]. Give the deadline if there is one. Keep the tone respectful and non-accusatory. Bold "suspended", any deadline, and the required deliverables. Use the sign-off "Kind regards, [Your name]".
What to change:
  • Anything in [square brackets] is a placeholder — swap it for the real detail each time.
  • The workflow set is an example from one team's process — adapt the references, email address and steps to yours, or skip it.

Tip: the Title is what you'll see in your prompt list, so keep it short and clear.

Before you hit send

  • Always read the draft. AI writes confidently even when it's wrong — you are the editor.
  • Check the facts yourself — dates, deadlines, figures, names and references.
  • Fill in every [placeholder] before sending; don't leave brackets in the email.
  • Sensitive email? It switches to “Kind regards” automatically — good, but reread the tone.
  • Mind confidentiality — don't paste in more sensitive information than the task needs.
Set up once, use every day. Adjust anything to fit how you work.
