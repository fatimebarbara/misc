📈 Key Findings & Patterns

🎯 Clear Priority Hierarchy Emerges

Data Analysis dominates: 14 out of 26 respondents gave this a 5/5 rating (54%). The team sees immediate, high-value automation potential here. Consistency checks come in a close second (4.08 average). These structural, systematic tasks are where the team wants AI to take the lead.

⚠️ Governance & Judgment Tasks Show Hesitation

Legal background checks (3.20 average) and Q&A during submission (3.60 average) reveal cautious optimism. These are tasks where judgment, accountability, and human oversight matter. The wider spread in responses (std dev 1.65) suggests the team is still figuring out where to trust AI in high-stakes scenarios.

💡 Critical Insight: Humans Want Partnership, Not Replacement

One respondent's submitted feedback encapsulates the team's broader ethos: "I would not phrase it as 'let AI take over,' as all our tasks are our ultimate responsibility." The team isn't asking for automation; they're asking for intelligent assistance and quality improvement.

💡 Team Suggestions for AI-Assisted Work

Extracted from respondent feedback — concrete, actionable use cases that should inform training curriculum:

Assessment of tenders: Need clarity on legal boundaries and AI capabilities in complex compliance scenarios (medical devices, regulated sectors).

Tender specification support: Rephrasing, award criteria, deliverables pricing, consistency checks.

Document analysis tasks: Checking repetitive documents, summarizing background materials, extracting key points.

Communication: Formulating emails (especially tricky or sensitive ones), Q&A drafting during submissions.

Evaluation support: Flagging inconsistencies, compliance validation, performance indicators extraction, risk identification.

Report generation: Drafting individual reports, synthesizing into consensus reports, structured output creation.

🎯 Strategic Recommendations for Training Design

1. Build Modular, Role-Based Sessions

The enthusiasm spreads across different tasks for different roles. Offer modules for:

  • Data-focused roles: Advanced analysis, prompt engineering, data quality validation
  • Document-heavy roles: Summarization, consistency checking, compliance validation
  • Communication roles: Email drafting, Q&A support, report writing
2. Address Legal & Risk Concerns Head-On

The 3.20 average on legal checks reflects real governance concerns, not irrational fear. Dedicate a session to: What AI cannot do safely. Where humans must retain authority. Audit trails & compliance. Verification workflows. This builds trust with skeptics.

3. Identify & Empower Early Adopters

~8–10 enthusiasts gave 5/5 ratings. These are your peer champions. Train them first, deeper. Ask them to lead breakout sessions or peer teaching. They'll model responsible, excited adoption for the skeptics.

📊 Executive Summary

Your team is pragmatic and ethically grounded. They're not asking for wholesale automation; they're asking for intelligent assistance that respects human judgment and accountability.

The demand is clear: Data analysis (4.27/5) and consistency checking (4.08/5) are top priorities. Quick wins here will build credibility for more ambitious AI use cases.

The caution is legitimate: Legal and high-stakes tasks show hesitation (3.20–3.60) because they involve real risk. Training should validate these concerns and offer frameworks for safe delegation.

The opportunity is massive: 27 engaged respondents, multiple suggestions, genuine curiosity. Your team is ready to learn. Design training that respects their intelligence, addresses their concerns, and delivers tangible value—and you'll have champions, not skeptics.
